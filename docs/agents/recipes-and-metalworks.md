# Recipes, cast molds and metalworks

Crafting, cook, crush, melt, cast, alloy and work-station recipes, the kiln, casting, the bloomery and anvil, the work
stations and leatherworking, hell and the barrier.

### Recipes, cast molds and metalworks

Crafting recipes live in `assets/data/recipes/<name>.json` as `{ "result", "count", "ingredients":
[ { "item" | "tag", "count" } ] }` (DTO `RecipeDefinition.cs`, compiled by
`RecipeLoader.CompileRecipe` into a `CraftingRecipe`), campfire recipes in
`recipes/cook/<name>.json` as `{ "input", "result", "count", "cookSeconds" }`
(`CookRecipeDefinition.cs` -> `CookRecipe`, one recipe per input, enforced) and quern recipes in
`recipes/crush/<name>.json` (`CrushRecipe`, likewise one per input) and work-station recipes in
`recipes/<station>/<result>.json` (`forge` for the anvil, `scrape` for the fleshing beam, `cut` for the leather table) as
`{ "input", "inputCount", "result", "count", "hits", "byproduct": { "item", "count" }, "collectWith" }`
(`WorkRecipeDefinition.cs` -> `WorkRecipe`, one per result, `inputCount` and `hits` 1..64, `byproduct` and `collectWith`
optional; see "Work stations"). Every name resolves
like a loot entry, with `block/` and `item/` prefixes pinning the namespace (`ContentFiles.TryResolve`), so
blocks and items are interchangeable as ingredients, results, inputs and outputs. Craft files are
named by their result, cook files by their input, and a reverse recipe by
`<result>_from_<ingredient>`.

**Cast molds** are the worked example. `mold_pickaxe` / `mold_axe` are pressed from three mudballs
(`recipes/mold_pickaxe.json`, `mold_axe.json`), uncraft back into three
(`recipes/mudball_from_mold_*.json`) and fire into `fired_mold_pickaxe` / `fired_mold_axe` by
right-clicking a lit branch pile with the mold hotbarred (`BlockInteractions.TryCookOrCollect` ->
`CampfireManager.TryPlaceFood`, `recipes/cook/mold_*.json`); a fired mold never uncrafts. They are all
`hasItem` slab blocks whose models under `assets/models/block/` are generated by
`tools/generate-mold-models.ps1` and never hand-edited: the script reads each sprite's alpha
silhouette (`assets/textures/items/mold_*.png`, copies of the copper tools — silhouette input only,
referenced by no JSON, since a block's icon comes from its baked model via `BlockCubeCache`), maps
it onto the inner 12x12 of the 16-cell top, greedy-merges the rim and pocket cells into box elements
and emits `cullface` only on faces that sit on the cell boundary — a cullface culls against the
*world* neighbor, never a sibling element, so the pocket walls carry none and always draw. Its
constants `$SlabHeight` (4), `$PocketDepth`, `$RimCells` and `$AlphaThreshold` sit at the top of the
file, and re-running is byte-identical until the real sprites arrive. The blocks set
`collidesByHitbox` with a `0..0.25` slab hitbox whose height must equal `$SlabHeight/16` X; raw molds wear the `mud` tile, fired ones `fired_mud` —
a `stone.png` placeholder that lives in `assets/textures/blocks/`, never `blocks_material/`.
There are **fourteen** of them now — pickaxe, axe, ingot, sword, ring, dagger and hammer, raw and fired — all generated by the same
script from the same constants.

**Nothing is crafted from a molten metal's ingot.** An ingot is a storage form (cast in `mold_ingot`, melts back
to the same ml) and no crafting recipe takes one (`MetalworksRecipeTests.NoCraftingRecipeTakesAnIngotOfAMoltenMetal`).
Metal reaches an item by being cast (iron, never molten, by being forged on the anvil instead): the heads (pickaxe, axe, sword), the ring (`copper_ring`, `tin_ring`, `bronze_ring`,
200 ml each, one `mold_ring` for all three metals) `copper_dagger` (`mold_dagger`, 200 ml) and `bronze_hammer_head` (`mold_hammer`, bronze only, 400 ml; the `bronze_hammer` is that head + branch + 2 vines). The ring doubles as the
generic metal part for everything else made of copper, tin or bronze: the bronze armor (cap 5, tunic 8, pants 7, boots 4,
gloves 4 rings), `bronze_chest` (8 rings and 4 planks), `clock` (4 copper rings and a mud brick), `lantern`,
`crystal_staff`, `alloy_bricks` (a copper and a tin ring) and the `focus_ring` / `reserve_charm` (2 rings and a vine).

**Tin** (`cassiterite_ore`, `TinGenerator`) is the second, weaker metal: commoner than malachite, in the
`TinMinY..TinMaxY` band, mined with any pickaxe; it crushes in the quern into `crushed_tin`, melts as `tin`
(ingot and crushed 100 ml, axe, pickaxe, sword and ring 200 ml; the raw ore does not melt, only crushed ore does) and casts into the five molds. Tin and
copper each have an axe, pickaxe and sword (`attack: "sword"`), the tool ladder runs primitive < tin < copper
< bronze.

**Bronze** is the alloy (3 copper + 1 tin by ml, made only in the alloy furnace, see the recipe kind
below) and the top of that ladder: an ingot (100 ml), ring, axe, pickaxe and sword heads (each 200 ml, melting back
to `bronze`, cast in the same five molds) and the three tools crafted like the others (head + branch + 2
vines), in the `axe` and `pickaxe` tags. It has no ore, no blocks and no armor. Its art is recolored from the copper tools; `MiningTests.BronzeOutranksCopperAndMinesWhatCopperMines`
holds the ladder as relations and `visual-examples/bronze.json` (`VOXEL_TIME=147`) shows the tin,
copper and bronze tools side by side.

**The casting basin** (`casting_basin`, fired on a campfire from `casting_basin_raw`, which is
pressed from four mudballs) is the mold that makes **blocks**: a hand-authored 8 px bowl (a 2 px
floor, a 2 px rim) whose model `assets/models/block/casting_basin.json` is the source of truth for
`CastingRenderer.Basin`, the `Pocket` the render pass uses for any cast whose result is a block
(`CastingRenderer.PocketFor`; item casts keep the mold slab's `Mold` pocket, and
`CastingTests.BasinPocketMatchesItsBakedModelAndBlockCastsUseIt` pins the tie). Nothing else
changed: `CastingManager` keys on the mold block id, so `recipes/cast/glass.json` (mold
`block/casting_basin`, fluid `glass`, 100 ml, result `block/glass`) is all it takes. **Molten
glass** (`data/molten/glass.json`) is the first non-copper material: `recipes/melt/sand.json` melts
a sand block into 100 ml (one kiln melt chunk, so an 800 ml crucible makes eight glass) and
`recipes/melt/glass.json` melts a glass block back into the same 100 ml.

**Molten metal is content, not a fluid.** It never enters `FluidManager`, never occupies a cell
and has no block: `assets/data/molten/<name>.json` declares a `MoltenMaterial` as
`{ "name", "texture", "color": [r, g, b] }`, where `texture` is an atlas tile (copper reuses
`lava_still`) and the color tints it. Ids are handed out **1-based in file-name order** (0 means
"no material"), and the tile is resolved in `BakeModels`, so `MoltenMaterial.Tile` is 0 until
then. `ContentRegistry.MoltenMaterialByName` / `MoltenMaterialById` are the lookups.

Two recipe kinds hang off that. `recipes/melt/<input>.json` is
`{ "input", "fluid", "amountMl" }` — what a block or item melts into and how much, one recipe per
input (`MeltRecipe`, `ContentRegistry.MeltRecipeForInput`). `recipes/cast/<result>.json` is the
metalworks recipe proper, `{ "mold", "fluid", "amountMl", "coolSeconds", "result", "count" }`,
keyed by the **(fired mold block, material)** pair, which must be unique (`CastRecipe`,
`CastRecipeFor(moldId, materialId)`, and `IsCastMold(id)` for "is this cell a mold at all").
Every shipped copper form — crushed copper (the raw malachite chunk does not melt), the ingot and both tool heads —
has a melt recipe, so **a cast melts back into exactly what it took**: `MetalworksRecipeTests`
asserts that conservation as a relation rather than pinning any ml figure.

A third kind, `recipes/alloy/<name>.json`, is `{ "result", "parts": [ { "fluid", "ml" }, ... ] }`
(`AlloyRecipe`, `ContentRegistry.AlloyRecipes`, compiled by `RecipeLoader.CompileAlloyRecipe`): one
full set of the parts becomes exactly their summed ml of the result, so alloying conserves metal by
construction. A recipe needs at least two distinct parts, none of them the result, and a metal may
be a part of one alloy only. `bronze.json` ships: 600 ml copper + 200 ml tin = 800 ml bronze
(`data/molten/bronze.json`), which is why the
crucible holds 800 ml: one vessel takes exactly one set (`AlloyKilnTests.OneCrucibleHoldsAFullSetOfEveryAlloy`).

**Iron** is smelted solid-state, never molten (see "The bloomery and the anvil" below). `hematite_ore` needs a
tier-2 (copper) pickaxe (`tools: {"pickaxe": 2}`, so the primitive and tin pickaxes whiff and copper and bronze mine it)
and drops a hematite chunk (the `hematite_ore` item), which the quern crushes into `crushed_iron`
(`recipes/crush/hematite_ore.json`). There is no melt recipe and no molten iron, so a kiln refuses every iron form
(`MetalworksRecipeTests.IronCrushesInTheQuernButNeverMelts`); the bloomery turns crushed iron into a `bloom`, which no
crafting recipe takes (`IronChainTests.NoCraftingRecipeTakesABloomItIsForgedOnTheAnvil`; there is no wrought iron): blooms
are stacked on the anvil and forged there (`recipes/forge/`, each recipe hammered out in its own `hits`, every bloom leaving
one `slag`) into the heads (`iron_pickaxe_head` 3, `iron_axe_head` 3,
`iron_sword_head` 4), the five-piece iron armor set (`iron_cap` 5, `iron_tunic` 8, `iron_pants` 7, `iron_boots` 4, `iron_gloves`
4, `sets/iron.json`; bronze's ring counts and one more armor point each) and `iron_ring` (2). The tools are still crafted
from a forged head (`iron_pickaxe`, `iron_axe`, `iron_sword`, each head + branch + 2 vine). The `iron_pickaxe` (power 3, above bronze's 2.5; in the
`pickaxe` tag) is the tool the hell barrier needs (plans/LAYERS.md), so hell is now reachable in survival
(`MiningTests.IronOutranksBronzeAndMinesEverythingBronzeMines`).

**Vulcanite** is the tier above iron, worked on the same anvil with no new mechanic. `vulcanite_ore` (hardness 60, above the
basalt it veins; `tools: {"pickaxe": 3}`, so the iron pickaxe and up) drops a `vulcanite_shard` (its loot table). The shard is
a forge metal only because a forge recipe takes it: 3 shards forge into `refined_vulcanite` (`recipes/forge/refined_vulcanite.json`),
and the refined metal forges into `vulcanite_pickaxe_head` 3, `vulcanite_axe_head` 3 and `bleeding_edge_head` 4; no crafting
recipe takes either metal. The tools are crafted like iron's (head + branch + 2 vine): `vulcanite_pickaxe` and `vulcanite_axe`
(power 4, in the `pickaxe`/`axe` tags) and `bleeding_edge` (the vulcanite sword), every stat above iron's; no armor. Like the other hand tools they `cinder_sword` (24 ember shards, 30 basalt and 1 refined vulcanite, no forge step) hits for twice the Bleeding Edge's base damage and sets its target burning (`onHit`).
have no durability. **Worldgen** (`VulcaniteGenerator`, `Stage.Vulcanite`, tuning `Vulcanite*` in `WorldGenerationConfig`): the
`OreNoiseLattice` deposits of a noise of their own (seed offset 186), stamped over **basalt only** right after hell's stamp, so they
lie in the barrier band and the Magma Caverns' skin, never above `BarrierTopY`, and no hell tree or ruin is veined. The threshold
`VulcaniteThreshold` (almost nothing passes it) drops by up to `VulcaniteNearLavaShift` times `VulcaniteGenerator.LavaProximity`, a
pure field: in hell 1 at the layer's lava level falling to 0 `VulcaniteLavaReachBlocks` rows up (`HellGenerator.RowsAboveLava`;
basalt lines only the all-lava Magma Caverns, so that level is the lava's surface), in the band 1 in the deep lava pockets' patches
(`LavaGenerator.PatchValue`) fading to 0 `VulcanitePatchMargin` under their edge. So the ore gathers by the pools. `HasOre` is the
stamp's point query on basalt (`VulcaniteGenerator.Over`). `VulcaniteTests`, `IronChainTests` (the shard is refined before anything
is forged of it, and every forge recipe is reachable), `MiningTests` (the ore's gate, the tier over iron);
`visual-examples/vulcanite.json` (`VOXEL_TIME=147`) films the ore beside a lava pool. The shard, ingot (refined vulcanite),
pickaxe and axe sprites and the ore tile are committed art; the three heads and the sword are placeholders from
`tools/generate-vulcanite-sprites.py` (USER_TODO.md).

**The cave ores** (`CaveOres`, plans/CAVE_BIOMES.md CB-2) are three ores found only in one cave sub-biome each, which drop
themselves: `amethyst_ore` in crystal caves and `jade_ore` in fungal caves (copper pickaxe and up) and `cinnabar_ore` in the
magma-touched deep (iron pickaxe; `MiningTests.TheCaveOresGateByTheirDepth`). They have no use yet.

**The barrier** (the `basalt` block, `BarrierGenerator`) seals the overworld from hell: `requiresTool` with
`tools: {"pickaxe": 3}`, so only the iron pickaxe mines it, hardness 40, and it drops itself (no loot
table, so a player may place it back). Worldgen lays it over `BarrierBottomY..BarrierTopY`: a gapless
core (`BarrierFullBottomY..BarrierFullTopY`) where every cell becomes barrier, carved air, water and
lava included, so no cave lets a player through; over the two ramps its share rises linearly from 0 at
the band's edge to 1 at the core, a ramp cell is barrier where a 3D OpenSimplex noise (seed offset 162,
`BarrierPatchFrequency`) mapped to 0..1 is under that share, and only solid, occluding ground is
replaced there, so caves stay open and the edges break into patches. The stamp runs last of all the
features (`Stage.Barrier`), so nothing but its vulcanite veins (`Stage.Vulcanite`, see "Vulcanite") writes over it; `BarrierGenerator.IsBarrier` is the field it
writes, bit for bit across chunk seams. Hell starts right below the band (see "Hell" next).
`BarrierTests`;
`visual-examples/barrier.json` (`VOXEL_TIME=147`) frames a ramp cave and the core's faces.

**Hell** (`WorldGen/Hell/HellGenerator.cs`, plans/LAYERS.md) is every cell at or below `HellTopY`, and
it has no bottom. It is a stack of layers `HellLayerPeriodBlocks` tall repeating downward forever
(`HellGenerator.LayerIndex`, 0 right under the barrier, the cell twin of `LayerClimate.HellLayerIndex`):
the top `PocketHeight` rows of a layer are its cavern pocket, the bottom `HellSlabThicknessBlocks` an
unbroken slab of `hellrock` (hardness 24, `tools: {"pickaxe": 3}`, so only the iron pickaxe digs down;
drops itself). A pocket cell opens where a two-octave 3D OpenSimplex noise (seed offset 163,
`HellCavernFrequency`, y scaled by `HellCavernYScale`), sampled on a world-aligned `HellNoiseStride`
lattice and blended trilinearly at the cell's center (`OreNoiseLattice.Trilinear`), exceeds the row's
threshold (`RowThreshold`): the layer's `OpenThreshold`, lower (more open) each layer down to
`HellOpenThresholdMin`, plus an edge bias (`HellEdgeStrength`, quadratic over `HellEdgeBlocks`) that
closes the cells toward the pocket's ceiling and floor, so both follow the noise. A row whose threshold
reaches `RowSolidLimit` is hellrock by definition and never samples. An open cell fewer than
`LavaDepth(layer)` cells above its pocket's floor (`HellLavaBaseDepth`, rising `HellLavaDepthPerLayer`
a layer to `HellLavaMaxDepth`) is a pool cell (`HellGenerator.InPool`), so the floor's dips pond and
deeper layers pond more: lava where the column's pool holds lava, else the biome's soil topped by its
surface (a dry, flat floor). The level is flat across a layer, the biome and pool are constant down a
column of one layer and a dry pool is solid, so every lava cell's sides and bottom are lava or solid and
nothing ever needs waking (a generated hell chunk's fluid frontier is empty).

**Hell biomes** (`WorldGen/Hell/HellBiomes.cs`, `HellBiome`, `HellBiomePalette`; L-7/L-8) are their own
enum, never the surface `Biome` (whose ordinals ride the column codecs): `Ashlands`, `MagmaCaverns` and
`FungalGrove`. `HellBiomes.At(x, y, z)` reads a two-octave 3D noise (seed offset 164,
`HellBiomeFrequency`) at the layer's pocket middle row, on a chunk-aligned 2D lattice per layer
(`HellBiomeLatticeStride`) blended bilinearly, plus a hashed lean per layer (`HellBiomeLayerBias`), cut at
`HellAshlandsMaxValue` / `HellFungalMinValue`: regions a few hundred blocks across, mixed within a layer,
laid out anew in the next, constant down a column of one layer (floor and ceiling agree). Each biome's
palette (blocks resolved once in `HellBiomes`' static constructor) names its floor `Surface` and `Soil`,
`Wall`, `Ceiling`, lava `PoolBed`, a `CeilingLight` (one ceiling cell right over open cavern in
`CeilingLightRate`, by a cell hash, `HellBiomes.Picks`), an optional `FloorLight` and the `LavaShare` of
its pool columns: 1 for the Magma Caverns (basalt, magma floors and beds), `HellAshlandsLavaShare` (ash
over cinder soil, scorched hellrock walls) and `HellFungalLavaShare` (fungal moss over fungal soil); a
partial share picks lava columns by a single-octave pool noise (seed offset 165, `HellPoolFrequency`, on
an `HellPoolLatticeStride` lattice). The skins (`HellGenerator.Classify`, one rule over two fields): a
solid pocket cell with open cavern within `HellFloorSkinBlocks` above is floor (surface right under air,
soil deeper, the pool bed under lava), else with open cavern within `HellCeilingSkinBlocks` below ceiling,
else (above the lava level) beside open cavern wall; the slab stays hellrock. The stamp computes the open
field once over the chunk plus a one-cell ring and the skin reach (`FillOpenMask`) from the same lattice,
rows and fraction expressions as `HellGenerator.IsOpen`, and caches the row rules and each column's biome
and pool per layer, so `HellGenerator.BlockAt` (the point field) equals the stamp bit for bit across
seams. Every palette block is solid and steady, so lava stays put. The lights glow in two palette colors
of their own: ember (`ember_crystal` 15, `magma_block` 9) and fungal teal (`fungal_glow` 14), five of the
eight `BlockLightPalette` slots then used (the sky's `crystal_leaves` take a sixth, pale cyan). The hell blocks (`hellrock`, `ash`, `cinder_soil`,
`scorched_hellrock`, `basalt`, `magma_block`, `ember_crystal`, `fungal_moss`, `fungal_soil`,
`fungal_glow`) are plain JSON cubes; the soils and
ash mine by hand, the rocks need any pickaxe, hellrock the iron one. Costs about 0.26 ms a warm hell
chunk with the flora below (0.15 before it), plus the hell trees grown on a cache miss, about 0.6 ms a
chunk on the probe's cold samples, which regrow every tree (`WorldgenCostProbe`, plans/PERF.md). The layer index also scales the hell mobs (see "Hell mobs").
`HellTests`, `HellBiomeTests`; `visual-examples/hell.json` (`VOXEL_TIME=147`) frames layers 0, 1 and 4
and a spot in each biome.

**Hell flora** (`L-9`; `WorldGen/Hell/HellFlora.cs`, `HellTrees.cs`, `HellTreeSpace.cs`) is per biome,
named in its palette: `GroundPlant` and `GroundPlantRate`, `VineRate` (0 for none) and the `Tree`
species with its `TreeChance` (the Fungal Grove: `glow_fern`, thick vines, `fungal_tree`; the Ashlands:
`ember_bloom`, sparse vines, `charred_tree`; the Magma Caverns none). The small flora is part of hell's
one rule: `HellGenerator`'s open cell above the lava level asks `HellFlora.AirCell` over the same
`IHellField` (point or stamp), so `BlockAt` still equals the stamp bit for bit and a chunk reads only
itself (the stamp's open mask reaches `HellFlora.ReachUp` = `HellVineMaxLength` rows above the chunk for
the vines). An air cell is a **vine** (`hell_vine`) when the first closed cell over it, at most
`HellVineMaxLength` up, is a ceiling cell whose salted cell hash (`HellBiomes.Hash`) hangs a vine at least
that long (1..`HellVineMaxLength`, stopping at the floor), so every vine cell hangs from a solid cell or
the vine over it; else an air cell over its biome's `Surface` (a floor's top or a dry pool's, never the
floor light) is now and then its `GroundPlant`. Flora only ever takes pocket air. **Trees** grow over
that and do not go through `TreeGenerator` (hell has no heightmap): hell is cut into
`HellTreeCellBlocks` squares (8, dividing the chunk), and each cell of each layer roots at most one
tree, a pure function of (cell, layer, seed): a hash picks the root column at least `HellTreeCellMargin`
inside the cell, the column's biome the species and chance, a hash one of the column's floors in the
layer (`HellGenerator.IsCavernAir` scans, its lattice samples memoized), and the root must stand on the
biome's `Surface`. It grows to maturity in a `HellTreeSpace`: free only in its own cell's columns and
in pocket air before the flora, so trees never meet, never enter rock, and the tree's cells all lie in
its chunk column. The shape is cached per key (`HellTrees`' `GenerationalCache`,
`HellTreeCacheCapacity`, cleared by `WorldSeed.Apply`) and `HellTrees.Stamp` (`Stage.HellTrees`, after
the hell stamp on both the wholly-hell and the mixed path) writes each tree's cells with their state
bytes over whatever flora stood there, so a tree is the same whichever chunk stamps it, and a vine cut by
a tree cell stays held by it. The species are ordinary tree JSON with no `worldgen` block (so
`TreeGenerator` never plants them): `fungal_tree` (oak logic, a pad cap of `fungal_cap`, an opaque-tiled
leaves block that, like every leaf block, passes light, casts no AO and hides no face, glowing teal at 9
in the fungal glow's palette slot, and decays like leaves) on
`fungal_log`, and the leafless `charred_tree` on `charred_log`. The plants are cross models
(`glow_fern` teal light 6, `ember_bloom` ember light 5, both `needsSupport`), and `hell_vine` is
`support: "above"`: each cell is held by the one over it, a solid ceiling or a vine, so a vine cut
anywhere drops what hangs below, through mining's recursive pop and, with this change, the blast scan
(`TntManager.PopIfUnsupported` follows a popped block's dependents past the blast box) and leaf decay
(`LeafDecayManager.PopUnsupportedAround` recurses), with no new block flag. Every tile is committed art, except the charred and fungal tree tiles, which are placeholders recorded in `USER_TODO.md`.
`HellFloraTests`; `visual-examples/hell-flora.json` (`VOXEL_TIME=147`) frames the Fungal Grove and the
Ashlands.

**The kiln** (`Simulation/KilnManager.cs`, keyed by the structure's anchor cell) is the furnace half.
How a kiln behaves is its anchor block's `KilnProfile` (the `kiln` object, `BlockType.Kiln`), which
`KilnState.Profile` caches from `KilnState.AnchorId` whenever a kiln is registered, restored or
mirrored (`KilnManager.ProfileOf`; never saved or sent, so the state, `stations.json` and
`StationNetCodec` are shared by every kiln size). Right-clicking it with an item declaring
`fuel.burnSeconds` stores a unit, up to the profile's `FuelCap` of **one** kind; a firestarter only lights a kiln that has fuel
(`CanIgnite`), and igniting spends the first unit at once, so four coal burn for four minutes and
then go out (lumber, 8 of which craft one oak plank via `oak_planks_from_lumber`, burns 15 s a unit). A crucible (crafted from mud bricks) right-clicked in stands in the hearth, and
anything with a melt recipe right-clicked after that
charges the vessel with **pending** metal — gated by `KilnManager.CanAccept`, which needs room
(every metal, molten and pending, counted together) and a metal that may join the vessel
(`AlloyRules.CanMix`): a **plain kiln** (`canAlloy` false) keeps a vessel to **one metal** and refuses
another while any of the first is in it, molten or pending; an **alloy kiln** (`canAlloy` true) also
lets the parts and the result of one alloy share it, charged in any order. Charging needs no fire. A
lit kiln then turns `KilnManager.MeltChunkMl` (the same for every kiln) of one metal's pending
charge molten every `KilnProfile.MeltSeconds` of its profile (the base 2 s over its `meltSpeed`): the
lowest-id metal that may melt (`AlloyRules.NextToMelt`, `KilnManager.MeltingMaterial`). In an alloy
kiln a part **waits**, solid, while another part of its alloy is in the vessel, and each lit tick
first turns every full set of parts, molten or pending, into the same ml of the result as pending
charge (`AlloyRules.Resolve`), which then melts at the kiln's pace: so 600 copper + 200 tin become
800 molten bronze in eight chunks, the surplus of an unfinished set waits for the rest, and the ml in
always equals the ml out. A lone metal melts in an alloy kiln as in a plain one; a vessel mixed in an
alloy kiln and carried to a plain one melts each metal on its own there. Melting **pauses**
rather than resets when the fire dies or the vessel is lifted out: `KilnState.MeltLeft` simply
stops ticking, so relighting resumes the partial chunk. `MeltProgress01` and `DisplayMoltenMl` are
what the render pass reads and neither depends on `Lit`, which is what keeps the drawn pool rising
smoothly between chunks and stops it dropping the instant the last unit burns out.
`DisplayPendingMl` is their conservation complement (drawn pending + drawn molten = `TotalMl`),
and `KilnRenderer.PendingScale01` turns it into the lump of the last input
(`CrucibleComponent.PendingInputId`) hovering over the rim, full-size while a whole unit is solid
and shrinking to nothing as the last unit melts — so the charge is never invisible and never cuts
out. The pool is every molten metal together, tinted as `KilnManager.PoolMaterial` (the metal with the
most molten ml), and the tooltip of a carried crucible lists each metal's molten and unmelted ml.
Breaking the structure drops the stored fuel and the crucible at the mined cell — the metal
inside is lost.

**The bloomery and the anvil** (`Simulation/Smithing/`, namespace `VoxelEngine.Simulation.Smithing`, tuning in
`BloomeryConfig`) make iron. `SmithingStations` owns both registries as one unit (`World.Smithing`), so the frame, the
save and the station broadcast each plumb one object. The flow is crushed iron + fuel -> **bloomery** (kept hot by
landing on a **bellows** at one of its tuyeres) -> `bloom` -> blooms stacked on the **anvil**, a recipe picked with the
smithing hammer's right-click and hammered out with its left (**the forge**, below) -> the part or armor + `slag`. There
is no wrought iron: the bloom is the forge metal.
- **The bloomery** (`bloomery` / `bloomery_part`, a 2x2x2 `multiblock` pressed from `bloomery_bricks` by the same
  bare-hand conversion; a stepped shaft with a tapping arch in front and an open throat on top, whose arch, throat and
  prop spots are `BloomeryLayout`, mirroring `models/block/bloomery.json`) is declared by a
  `bloomery` object on the anchor block: `{"oreCap", "fuelCap", "ore", "bloom", "tuyeres"}` (`BloomeryProfile`, parsed by
  `BlockSchemaParser.ParseBloomery`, `BlockType.Bloomery`; the two names resolve to ids at load and a missing or unknown
  one throws naming the file; `tuyeres` is at least one `[x, y, z]` cell in the authored, north-facing local space, each
  just outside a side face of the volume, refused otherwise; it is only legal on a multiblock anchor and never with
  `kiln`). It does **not** reuse `KilnProfile`/`KilnLayout`, which carry crucible semantics. `BloomeryManager` (keyed by
  anchor, main thread only) copies the kiln's pattern: `Ensure`, `Update` as the integrity sweep (a blast reverts the
  structure and drops what it held), `Capture`/`Restore` (`StationSaveData.Bloomeries`), `WriteNet`/`ReadNet`,
  `NotifyBlockRemoved`. Right-click (`BlockInteractions.TryBloomeryInteract`, in the chain after the kiln) loads the
  profile's ore and any fuel one unit at a time, a firestarter lights it (`IItemUseWorld.CanIgniteKiln`/`IgniteKiln` cover
  both structures, so a fire spell does too) once it holds ore and fuel, an empty hand collects the finished blooms, and
  no new ore goes in before that. **Heat model** (`BloomeryRules`, GL-free): a lit bloomery loses `HeatDecayPerSecond` of
  its 0..1 heat a second; at or over `BandMin` the unit progresses one second a second (`ProgressRate`, the same at full
  heat: **nothing is too hot**, every finished unit of `SecondsPerOre` is a bloom and the fuel burns at one rate), and
  under it the unit's progress **runs back**, linearly faster the colder the fire, up to `ColdReverseRate` for a dead one,
  never under 0 and never losing the ore. A jump on the spot onto the bellows once per `PumpCooldownSeconds` (or a
  second) beats the decay (`BloomeryRulesTests` asserts the relation).
- **The bellows** (`bellows`, a plain cube for now with placeholder tiles, crafted from oak planks and hide) carries the
  `"bellows": true` flag (`BlockType.IsBellows`). It **gives under a weight**: `"sink": 0.5` makes it a sink block, so a
  player's body settles slowly into its top half (`SinkLayer`, the same rule on a client and a server), and
  `BellowsSquash` (`SmithingStations.Bellows`, display only, fed every player's feet by `LocalPlayer.UpdatePlayerModel`)
  holds the bag down to the lowest feet standing in it and swells it back at `BloomeryConfig.BellowsExpandSpeed` once
  they leave. While it is not a full block its cell sits in `ChunkManager.SwingingCells`, so the mesher leaves it out
  (the bellows never joins the greedy pass for that) and `SmithingPasses` draws its cube squashed; it sets
  `occludes: false`, since a squashed bag must not hide the face of the block beside it. Only player bodies sink, so a
  mob standing on it does not press it down (`BellowsSquashTests`, `visual-examples/bellows-squash.json`). It works only from a **tuyere**: the cells the profile names, turned with
  the structure's facing (`BloomeryProfile.IsTuyere`, through `MultiblockShape.RotatePoint`); the shipped bloomery's two
  sit beside the back lower cell of each side wall, where `models/block/bloomery.json` stands a clay nozzle out of the
  inset hearth tier to the face of the volume, its end the `bloomery_tuyere` tile (`BloomeryLayoutTests` holds a nozzle
  to every tuyere cell). Heat comes only from a body
  **landing** on it: `World.PressBellows(feet, halfWidth, fall)` finds the bellows a hair under the feet
  (`BellowsProbe`: the middle, then the footprint's corners) and calls `BloomeryManager.PressBellows(cell, fall)`, which
  adds `BloomeryRules.LandingHeat(fall)` (nothing under `BellowsMinFallBlocks`, so a settle or an auto-step never pumps;
  `BellowsBaseHeat` plus `BellowsHeatPerBlock` a block fallen, capped at `BellowsMaxHeat`) to every lit bloomery with a
  tuyere at that cell whose own `PumpCooldownSeconds` has run out. The callers are the authoritative simulation only, never
  a client: the local player's `FallTracker` landing (`LocalPlayer`, beside the fall damage; not in water), a server
  player's accepted state (`ServerPlayer.ObserveAcceptedState` returns the landing's drop, `PlayerSessions.PressOnLanding`;
  `ServerPlayerLandingTests`), and every non-flying mob's dry landing (`Mob.NoteLanding` keeps the fall for
  `Mob.TryTakeBellowsLanding`, taken in `World.Update` apart from the cosmetic landing dust). Item drops never press. A later
  automation (a mechanical bellows, automated feeding) is `P-59` in plans/TODO.md; `PressBellows` takes any source.
- **The anvil** (`anvil`, a plain model block made of cobblestone so the chain can bootstrap) is a **work station**
  (see "Work stations" below) with `"workstation": {"tool": "hammer", "recipes": "forge", "surface": 0.9375}`: forge
  metal (any item a `recipes/forge/` recipe takes, `ContentRegistry.IsWorkInput("forge", id)`: blooms, vulcanite shards and
  refined vulcanite) stacks on it, a right-click with a member of the `hammer` tag (the `bronze_hammer`, bronze-headed,
  `tool.hitDelayMs` 2500, so there is no spamming) opens its recipe list, and the hammer's hits forge the pick, dropping
  its result and `byproduct` (a bloom's slag, one per bloom) on top of the anvil. Recipes are data
  (`recipes/forge/<result>.json`, see the top of this file), so another solid metal plugs in with its own files.
- **Persistence and the wire.** The bloomeries save into `stations.json` (`Bloomeries`) beside the work stations
  (`Anvils`, see "Work stations") and ride the **end** of the `StationState` frame, after the cast molds
  (`World.WriteStations`, `ClientMirror.ApplyNetworkStations`); a bloomery carries no overheat or slag. A client's copies
  are display-only mirrors that never tick.
- **Look and feel.** `RenderManager.Smithing.RenderSmithing` (`SmithingPasses`) draws the ore charge as a pile in the
  throat, its top piece shrinking as the unit smelts (`BloomeryLayout.ChargeStack`), the fuel as a bed on the hearth
  inside the arch, the finished blooms piled at the arch's front (self-lit while the fire still smelts), and an anvil's
  stack as one face-up piece per unit, each resting on the one below and turned a little against it. A lit bloomery's
  hearth and throat glow with an ember bed whose brightness follows the heat (`BloomeryRules.GlowAlpha`, dull under the
  band, full at `BloomeryConfig.GlowFullHeat`), it smokes out of its throat, and a jump in its heat (a landing on the
  bellows, or lighting it) throws a burst of sparks out of the throat (`Particles/FurnaceEffects`, `BlastSparks`; it
  reads the heat alone, so a client's mirror plays it with nothing on the wire). A lit bloomery joins
  `AmbientEffects.FireCells`, so it throws embers and a point light.
  The look-at label shows a status line (`LookLabelState.Detail`, `SmithingReadout`): a lit bloomery's heat with
  `too cold` / `good`, its ore and smelt percentage, a done run's blooms, or a work station's line (see "Work
  stations").
- **Slag** (`slag`, the byproduct of every bloom hammered into a part) crafts into `slag_bricks`, a plain building block.
- `IronChainTests` (the chain's relations, including that the anvil, hammer and bellows need no iron, that no crafting
  recipe takes a bloom and that every forge recipe is reachable), `BloomeryRulesTests`, `BloomeryManagerTests` (no

  `visual-examples/bloomery.json` (`VOXEL_TIME=147`, the `bloomery` op's `bellows`, the `anvil` op's `select`/`hits`

**Work stations** (`Simulation/WorkStations/WorkStationManager`, namespace `VoxelEngine.Simulation.WorkStations`) are
one mechanic behind the anvil, the fleshing beam and the leather table: any plain model block with a `workstation` object
`{"tool", "recipes", "surface"}` (`WorkStationProfile`, `BlockType.WorkStation`, parsed by
`BlockSchemaParser.ParseWorkStation`; `tool` is an item tag, checked once tags load, `recipes` a folder under `recipes/`
whose files `RecipeLoader.LoadWorkRecipes` loads for every folder some block names, `surface` the height its stack is
drawn at). Every work recipe (`WorkRecipe`, `Station` = its folder) uses the schema at the top of this file plus an
optional `"collectWith"` item, and one result is made by one recipe across all folders.
- **The stack.** A station holds a stack of one input (`State.InputId`/`InputCount`) and the recipe picked for it with its
  hits (`SelectedResultId`, `Hits`, `CooldownLeft`). Right-click (`BlockInteractions.TryWorkStationInteract`): an input of
  the station's folder in hand stacks one more (`TryStack`, refused for another input or at `StackCap`, the largest recipe
  of that input), and anything else, an empty hand included, takes one back (the whole stack while sneaking, `TryTake`; a
  pick the smaller stack no longer covers is forgotten with its hits); the station's tool never claims this click and a
  bare station none at all, so a block still places against it.
- **The pick and the hits.** The tool's right-click on a stacked station (`BlockInteractions.TryOpenWorkList`,
  `RightClickOutcome.Forge`) opens the recipe list (`App/ForgeInput`, `UI/Forge/ForgeView` + `ForgeLayout`, drawn by
  `ForgeRenderer` under the block's name: one card per recipe `WorkStationManager.Offered` gives the stack, that input's
  and no more than it holds). A card **picks** (`SmithingStations.Select` -> `TrySelect`; nothing leaves the stack yet),
  and the list closes on a pick, CLOSE, E, Escape, the pause menu, death, another item in hand, leaving reach or the stack
  running out. The tool's left button then works it (`LeftClickAction.UpdateWorkStationHit`, `Mining.IsStationTool`):
  `TryHit` needs a recipe to work (`State.Working`: the pick, or the only recipe offered, which the first hit picks, so a
  fleshing beam needs no list) and the gap since the last hit run down (`State.CooldownLeft`, ticked in `Update`, with
  `BloomeryConfig.HammerSlackSeconds` of jitter allowed on a server); the hit that reaches `hits` takes the input off the
  stack and drops the result and byproduct on top (`BlockInteractions.WorkStationHit`, `World.WorkStationHitFor`).
- **Waiting results.** A recipe with `collectWith` keeps its result on the station (`FinishedId`/`FinishedCount`, up to
  `FinishedCap`; a hit that would mix two results or overflow is refused) until a right-click with that item spends one and
  hands one over (`TryCollect`); nothing else takes it, and a station whose block is gone drops it back as its input.
- **Persistence and the wire.** Saved in `stations.json` as `Anvils` (`AnvilData`, the anvil's old names kept so older
  saves load: `Metal`/`Count` the stack, `Selected`/`Hits`/`Cooldown` the pick, `Finished`/`FinishedCount` the waiting
  result), clamped on load and a pick or waiting result that no longer resolves forgotten; on the wire after the
  bloomeries: block id, input id and count, pick, hits, cooldown, waiting result id and count (protocol **63**). Connected,
  a pick is predicted on the mirror and sent as `NetIntentKind.Forge(cell, result id)` and a hit predicted (from the
  mirrored `Working`) and sent as `NetIntentKind.Hammer(cell)` (`IntentReplay.ForgeFor`/`HammerFor`: the station's tool in
  hand, the player's own ray on it, and for a hit its gap run down); stacking, taking back and collecting ride `Use`.
- **Look.** `SmithingPasses` draws the stack on the station's surface as one face-up piece per unit, and a waiting result
  as a second pile beside it; the look-at line (`SmithingReadout.WorkStation`) names the work and its hits, the stack, and
  a waiting result with its collect item, and the station bar (`StationBars.WorkStation`) fills with the picked hits.
- `WorkStationManagerTests` (stack, take-back, offer, pick, auto-pick, hits, every recipe finishing on its own hits on its
  own station, waiting results, their drop-back, save and wire), `IronWorksWorldTests`, `LeatherWorksWorldTests`,
  `GameServerTests.AHammerHitIsJudgedByTheHammersGapOnTheServer`.

**Leatherworking** turns a hunted hide into the leather set in four quick, visible steps, the way the iron works do,
with no long timers: **skin -> scrape and salt -> tan -> cut**.
- **Skin.** Deer and boars drop `hide` (shown as Raw Hide; the id is kept for older saves) and the dire boar a pile of
  them (`loot/<species>.json`).
- **Scrape and salt** on the **fleshing beam** (`fleshing_beam`, a log on two legs, crafted from a log and planks), a work
  station with `{"tool": "knife", "recipes": "scrape"}`. Raw hides stack on it; a knife (`tags/knife.json`: the
  `flint_knife`, crafted from flint and a branch, and the copper and obsidian daggers) scrapes the one recipe
  (`recipes/scrape/salted_hide.json`) in a few strokes, and its `collectWith: item/salt` keeps the scraped hide on the
  beam until a right-click with **salt** takes it off as a `salted_hide`, one salt a hide.
- **Salt** comes from **rock salt** (`rock_salt`, any pickaxe, drops 1-3 salt), which `WorldGen/Features/RockSaltGenerator`
  lays in the top `WorldGenerationConfig.RockSaltDepth` cells of bare surface stone: the deep sea floor (an ocean column
  below the gravel band) and every soil-stripped column (`DirtDepth` 0: bare-rock crests, ravine walls), in patches of a
  2D noise (seed offset 189, `RockSaltFrequency`/`RockSaltThreshold`), replacing stone only so a carved cave stays open.
  It runs after the cave ores and before the water (`Stage.RockSalt`); the far LOD draws those cells as plain stone.
  Highlands start far from spawn, so early salt comes from the sea. `RockSaltTests`.
- **Tan** in the **tanning vat** (`tanning_vat`, an open plank tub), declared by a `tanning` object
  `{"input", "result", "count", "tannin", "water", "capacity", "seconds", "liquid"}` (`TanningProfile`,
  `BlockType.Tanning`, `BlockSchemaParser.ParseTanning`). Right-click (`BlockInteractions.TryVatInteract` ->
  `TanningVatManager.TryUse`): a water bucket fills a dry vat and turns back into a bucket, **bark** adds a tannin unit up
  to the capacity, salted hides join the batch while nothing tans or waits, and anything else collects the leather or
  takes back a batch that has not started. The batch tans (`TanningRules`) only with water and a bark unit per hide,
  pausing without them and never losing progress, and after `seconds` every hide becomes `count` leather while its bark
  and the water are spent. Bark is split off with the lumber when an axe splits a log on a stump (`loot/log_split.json`,
  `hollow_log_split.json`) and burns as a little fuel. The vat ticks with the works (`SmithingStations.Vats`), saves as
  `Vats` in `stations.json`, rides the `StationState` frame after the work stations (protocol **64**) and drops its
  batch, bark and leather when broken. `SmithingPasses` draws its water (`StationPasses.VatLiquid`, the still-water tile,
  brown once a batch tans), the hides floating in it and the leather on its floor; its look-at line is
  `SmithingReadout.Vat` and its bar `StationBars.Vat`.
- **Cut** on the **leather table** (`leather_table`, planks on four legs), a work station with
  `{"tool": "knife", "recipes": "cut"}`: leather stacks on it, the knife's right-click lists the patterns
  (`recipes/cut/leather_<piece>.json`) and its strokes cut the pick. No crafting recipe makes the leather set any more
  (the cotton and raw-hide recipes are gone), and the `bellows` takes leather.
- `LeatherChainTests` (each step feeds the next: hunting drops what the beam takes, rock salt drops what takes it off,
  splitting drops the vat's tannin, every leather piece is cut from the vat's leather, no crafting recipe takes a hide at
  any stage, and both leather stations' knife is crafted without iron), `TanningVatTests`, `LeatherWorksWorldTests`;
  `IronChainTests.EveryWorkRecipeIsReachableFromTheRawWorld` covers every station folder.

**The alloy furnace** (`alloy_furnace` / `alloy_furnace_part`, `multiblock.size` 3) is the second
kiln: a 3x3x3 hollow shell of `alloy_bricks` (one mud brick block, a copper and a tin ring each,
`recipes/alloy_bricks.json`) pressed by the
same bare-hand conversion, with `{"fuelCap": 8, "meltSpeed": 1.5, "canAlloy": true}`. It is the same
`KilnManager` state, save and wire shape; only its profile and its model differ. Where the render
pass (`StationPasses.RenderKilns`) draws the crucible, the pending lump and the fuel row, where the
fire burns (`KilnManager.LitCells`, the flame and point light `AmbientEffects` gathers) and where
the hearth's light is sampled are its `KilnLayout` (`KilnProfile.Layout`), which mirrors its model
`assets/models/block/alloy_furnace.json` (a plinth, a body between four corner buttresses, a copper
course, a stepped roof and a chimney; the firemouth x 10..38, y 5..24, z 0..36 in 0..48 model units
under a stepped vault, the crucible on a pedestal in its middle, the eight fuel units in two rows of
four, `KilnLayout.FuelSlotLocal`) the way `KilnLayout.Kiln` mirrors the mud brick kiln's (a beehive:
plinth, body, two daub dome tiers and a flue; the mouth x 8..24, y 4..19, z 0..20); the crucible bowl's
own sizes stay on `KilnRenderer`. A lit kiln of either kind glows with an ember bed on its hearth floor
(`KilnLayout.EmberLocal`/`EmberSize`, `StationPasses.DrawEmberBed`, tiled by `KilnRenderer.EmberGrid`)

and alloys it, and `visual-examples/multiblock-models.json` films all three structures cold and lit,
from the front, above and behind.

**Casting** (`Simulation/CastingManager.cs`, one `CastState` per mold cell) is the other half.
Right-clicking a fired mold with a crucible binds that tool instance and pours at
`CastingManager.PourMlPerSecond`, draining the stack a little every frame. An empty mold takes, of
the vessel's molten metals it has a cast recipe for, the one with the most ml (`CastingManager.PourRecipe`,
the lowest material id on a tie), and a half-filled one only more of the metal it was started with,
so a mixed crucible pours one metal and keeps the rest. The pour **pauses**
(losing nothing) when the crucible is deselected, carried out of `World.InteractionReach` or emptied
of that metal, and another right-click resumes it. The frame that completes
the fill starts the cooling instead of cooling in the same frame, so the two phases are never
ambiguous; `Cool01` then runs 0..1 over the recipe's `coolSeconds` and the liquid dissolves into
the result through `CastingRenderer.LiquidAlpha` / `ResultAlpha`, a complementary `MathUtils.SmoothStep`
pair that always sums to 1 (no hard cut at the end of the pour). A finished cast is picked up with
another right-click, and breaking the mold — **creative included** — drops a finished cast and only
a finished cast; metal still cooling is lost. Like the campfire, quern and kiln registries, cast
progress is saved with the world (`stations.json`, see "Saves"; a pour comes back paused and a right-click resumes it), and an unloaded mold cell freezes
rather than being forgotten. Hovering a mold shows its progress as a bar per cell (`StationBars.Mold`): a pour reads `Copper 23/100ml`, a cooling one `..., cooling` and a finished one `..., ready`, and an empty mold reads `0/<ml>ml` for each metal it casts (one bar per cast recipe, so the ring shows all three).

The right-click chain in `RightClickAction.TryUseSelected` runs `TryCookOrCollect` -> `TryQuernInteract` ->
`TryKilnInteract` -> `TryCastInteract` -> `TryToggleOpenable` -> `TryOpenChest` -> `TryOpenSpellTable` -> `TryOpenWaystone` -> `TryUseAltar` (all on `BlockInteractions`) -> a held spell staff's cast (which never places) -> the item's own `use` action or block placement, and the
first one that claims the click wins (`TryUseSelected(maxDistance, out RightClickOutcome)` hands back which). On a kiln: an empty hand lifts the crucible out into the hand, equipped exactly as a crucible lifted off the ground (and puts it
straight back when the tools pool is full), a crucible stands one in, a melt input charges it, fuel
goes into the hearth, a **firestarter falls through** to its own `Item.OnUse` so it can strike and
spend durability, and a **block in hand falls through** to placement against the structure — any
other click on a kiln is spent. On a mold: a crucible click is spent either way, so a refused pour
never places anything, while a block in hand still falls through and molds can be stacked against
each other. After the mold step comes `TryToggleOpenable`: a click on an openable block (a
trapdoor, either half of a door, which swings both) flips its open bit whatever is in hand, so
nothing places against it. Then `TryOpenSpellTable`: a click on a `spellBuilder` block with anything
but a block in hand is claimed as `RightClickOutcome.SpellTable` with no side effect and no swing (the
local player's `GameplayInput` opens the builder from that outcome; a server's replay changes nothing),
so a staff in hand never casts at the table. Then `TryOpenWaystone`: a click on either half of a whole waystone, unless sneaking or
holding a block (which place against it), is claimed as `RightClickOutcome.Waystone` the same way, its cell kept in
`RightClickAction.LastWaystoneCell`; the local player's `GameplayInput` hands it to `WaystoneInput.OpenFromStation`,
which discovers it and opens the waystone screen (see "Waystones"); a server replaying a client's `Use` discovers it for that
player instead (`World.LastWaystoneCell`, see "Waystones on a server"). Then `TryUseAltar`: a click on a battle tower's
altar, on the same terms, is claimed as `RightClickOutcome.Altar` and judged or begun through `World.UseAltar` (see
"Battle tower").
